Application of Learning

Incident scenarios

💻 ~20–25 min
How to answer each scenario Use the same structure we modelled together on Slide 6: What happened? → What type of attack? → What control was missing or failed? → What would you recommend? Complete at least one scenario fully — you're welcome to attempt more than one tier.
🟢 Foundation

Scenario A — The Urgent Invoice

Incident report Jai, an accounts assistant, receives an email that looks like it's from the finance manager, marked "URGENT — pay this invoice today". The sender's address is almost identical to the real one but has an extra letter. Jai clicks the attached "invoice" link and enters his email login to "view" it. Two days later, emails start being sent from Jai's account to other staff.
Think about which category this falls into — was it a technology gap, or something a person could have caught?
🟡 Developing

Scenario B — No Second Lock

Incident report A retail company's customer database is accessed using a password that had been leaked in an unrelated breach months earlier. The compromised account belongs to a warehouse staff member who does not have multi-factor authentication enabled, even though IT rolled out MFA for admin accounts last year. 40,000 customer records are copied before the access is noticed three weeks later.
Two things went wrong here, not one — a rollout decision, and something the password itself couldn't fix. What were they?
🟡 Developing

Scenario C — Site Down

Incident report A small online bookstore's website becomes unreachable during their biggest sale of the year. Server logs show an enormous spike in requests from thousands of different IP addresses in a short window, far beyond their usual traffic. The business has no traffic filtering or rate-limiting in place, and their hosting plan does not scale automatically.
🟣 Extending

Scenario D — The Long Game

Incident report A mid-sized engineering firm discovers that design files for a client project have appeared on a competitor's tender six months later. Investigation shows a former contractor's account was never deactivated after their contract ended four months ago. Logs show the account was used twice, from an unfamiliar location. The firm's offboarding process is a verbal handover between managers with no formal checklist, and file access was granted broadly to "all staff" rather than to the project team specifically.
Look for a technical gap, a procedural gap, and a permissions decision. Each is a separate weakness.
A good answer names a genuine downside of your choice, not just its benefits.
