Explicit Explanation
What counts as a "digital system"?
notes

A digital system is hardware, software and data working together to do a job for a user or an organisation. Every example in this lesson can be examined with the same two questions:

QUESTION 1

What function does it perform?

What is the system actually doing — what task is it carrying out?

QUESTION 2

What capability does that give the user?

What can a person or organisation now do that they couldn't do before?

Explicit Explanation
Five sectors of digital systems
full notes

Below are full notes on the five sectors covered in this lesson. Each entry includes the definition, the capability it gives users, and a table of real-world examples.

Assistive Technologies

Digital systems designed to remove a barrier that would otherwise stop someone doing something independently.

CAPABILITY IT GIVES Gives people independence and access to everyday tasks — communication, information, and movement — that a disability would otherwise block.
Copy this table
SystemWhat it does
Screen readersConverts on-screen text to speech for people who are blind or have low vision
Hearing aids with streamingBluetooth-enabled aids stream phone audio directly, cutting background noise
Eye-gaze controlTracks eye movement to operate a computer for users with limited mobility
Sensor-feedback prostheticsLimb sensors send signals back to the user, restoring a sense of grip or touch

Financial Services

Digital systems that let individuals and organisations move, manage and protect money without visiting a branch.

CAPABILITY IT GIVES Gives users speed (instant transfers), security (real-time fraud detection), and access (banking anywhere, anytime) that paper-based banking never could.
Copy this table
SystemWhat it does
Banking appsCheck balances, pay bills, and transfer money from a phone in seconds
Contactless / tap-to-payA single tap authorises a payment using encrypted card or device data
Fraud detectionFlags unusual spending patterns automatically, often before the user notices
Robo-adviceSoftware builds and manages an investment portfolio based on a user's goals

GPS & Location Systems

Digital systems that work out where something is in real time, using signals from satellites or networks.

CAPABILITY IT GIVES Gives organisations and individuals real-time positioning — enabling navigation, logistics planning, and the ability to find or track something exactly when it matters.
Copy this table
SystemWhat it does
Turn-by-turn navigationCalculates and updates a route in real time as conditions change
Fleet trackingLets a delivery company see every vehicle's location at once
Search and rescueLocates a missing hiker's phone or beacon signal to direct responders
GeofencingTriggers an action automatically when a device enters or leaves a set area

Robotics

Digital systems combined with physical machinery that sense, decide, and act in the real world.

CAPABILITY IT GIVES Gives organisations precision and scale beyond human physical limits — repeating a delicate or heavy task exactly, for hours, without fatigue.
Copy this table
SystemWhat it does
Warehouse picking robotsLocate and retrieve items from shelves to speed up order fulfilment
Surgical robotsTranslate a surgeon's hand movements into smaller, steadier instrument motion
Agricultural robots / dronesMonitor crop health or apply treatment across large fields automatically

Traffic Management

Digital systems that monitor and control the flow of vehicles across a road network, often city-wide.

CAPABILITY IT GIVES Gives a city the ability to optimise traffic flow and safety at a scale no human traffic controller could manage in real time.
Copy this table
SystemWhat it does
Adaptive traffic signalsAdjust light timing automatically based on real-time traffic volume
Congestion sensorsDetect slow-moving traffic and feed data into navigation apps
Incident detection camerasSpot a stopped vehicle or crash and alert responders automatically
