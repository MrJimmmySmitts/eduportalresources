2
Key Concept
What is Investigation?
Definition

Investigation means deliberately gathering information to understand a problem — before you start designing. It is purposeful, structured, and focused on specific questions about users and their needs.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Investigation is not just Googling. It involves understanding who has the problem, looking at what solutions already exist, finding gaps, and asking who is affected and how.

Two Types of Research

Type 1

Primary Research

You collect it yourself.

  • Surveys — questionnaires sent to real people
  • Interviews — direct conversation with users
  • Observations — watching how people interact with existing tools

Best for: understanding user feelings, preferences, and direct experiences.

Type 2

Secondary Research

Already collected by someone else.

  • Articles and news reports
  • Government or academic statistics
  • Existing app/product reviews
  • Research reports and studies

Best for: understanding the scale of a problem and what already exists.

3
Framework
Analysing Existing Solutions

Before designing anything new, you must understand what already exists — and why it falls short. Use these four questions to structure your investigation.

01

What exists?

Name at least 2–3 digital solutions already attempting to address your topic area. These might be apps, websites, hardware devices, automated systems, or AI tools.

02

Who uses it?

Describe the main users of these existing solutions. What problem does each solution try to solve for them? What is the context in which they use it?

03

What's missing?

What do existing solutions not do well? What complaints, limitations, or gaps appear in user reviews, news coverage, or research reports? Where do people still struggle?

04

Why does the gap matter?

Who is affected by this gap in existing solutions? How significant is the impact on these people? Is there evidence (statistics, examples, stories) that the gap causes a real problem?

Key Concept
Identifying Your Users

Identifying your users is one of the most important steps in investigation. Your design can only solve a problem if you understand who has the problem and what their experience is like.

⚠️ Keep It Focused

Your user group does not have to be every possible person. A focused, specific user group makes your investigation — and eventually your design — much stronger. "Students aged 13–18 with a visual impairment" is a better starting point than "anyone who uses the internet."

User Profile Template

Use these four headings to document your user group. Copy them into your paper workbook.

User Profile Field 1

Who are they?

Describe in 1–2 sentences: age group, role, setting, or context. Be specific enough that you could picture a real person.

User Profile Field 2

What problem do they face?

Describe the specific difficulty, frustration, or unmet need they experience. Use their perspective, not yours.

User Profile Field 3

What do they currently do?

How do they handle the problem now? What tools, workarounds, or manual processes do they use? What are the limitations of those approaches?

User Profile Field 4

What would make their life better?

What outcome would genuinely solve the problem for them? Focus on the result they want, not the specific technology.

5
Key Skill
Writing a Problem Statement

A problem statement is a clear, concise description of the problem your digital solution will address. It anchors your entire project — everything you design and build should connect back to it.

Problem Statement Formula
[USER GROUP] need a way to [ACCOMPLISH GOAL]
because [INSIGHT ABOUT THE PROBLEM]

Strong vs Weak — Examples

✅ Strong Problem Statement

"Elderly users in aged care facilities need a way to video call family members independently because existing platforms require too many steps and unfamiliar interfaces, causing frequent call failures and social isolation."

Why it works

It names a specific user group, describes a concrete goal, and provides a clear insight grounded in the users' real experience (not just a vague complaint about "bad apps").

❌ Weak Problem Statement

"People need a better app because the current apps are bad."

Why it fails

No specific user group. No specific goal. No evidence or insight — just a vague opinion. It gives you nothing useful to design from.
