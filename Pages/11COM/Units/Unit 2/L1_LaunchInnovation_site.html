The big idea

What drives innovation

Learning

Every piece of technology you use started as someone noticing a problem worth solving — Velcro, the microwave, bubble wrap, the seatbelt. Two things make that happen.

Curiosity

Noticing a problem, need or gap that most people walk straight past.

Ingenuity

Inventive, resourceful problem-solving — finding a workable way to address what you noticed.

Your end result this term can take any of these three forms — all equally valid:

Proof of concept

Shows the idea could work — doesn't need to be fully built.

Prototype

An early working version, rough around the edges.

Product

A more finished, polished solution.

Common misconception

You do not need a fully working app or finished product to succeed in this project. A genuine proof of concept, well documented and evaluated, is just as valid as a polished product.

A lens for finding problems

The UN Sustainable Development Goals

Learning

The UN Sustainable Development Goals are 17 global goals, agreed by almost every country, naming the problems most worth solving — from health and education to climate and inequality. They're a useful lens for finding a real problem your project could address.

Good Health & Wellbeing
Quality Education
Climate Action
Reduced Inequalities
Clean Water & Sanitation
Sustainable Cities

This is a sample of six — there are 17 in total. Search "UN Sustainable Development Goals" for the complete list if you want to explore further.

Example

"Clean Water & Sanitation" could inspire a low-cost sensor that detects unsafe drinking water — a real problem, solvable with a digital system.

A quick preview

What makes a solution "innovative"?

Learning

We'll go deeper on these in Week 3 — for now, just be aware of the four characteristics that will eventually shape how your solution is judged.

  • Originality — something genuinely new, not just a copy of an existing solution
  • Effectiveness — it actually solves the problem it was designed for
  • User-centred — built around what real users need, not just what's easy to build
  • Disruptiveness — changes how people approach the problem, not just a small improvement
