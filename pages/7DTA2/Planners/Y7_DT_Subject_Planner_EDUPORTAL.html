Victorian Curriculum Achievement Standard

By the end of Level 8, students select appropriate hardware for particular tasks. They explain how data is transmitted and secured in networks. Students identify and describe cyber security threats.

Students represent data using integers and binary. They acquire, manipulate and validate data using spreadsheets and single-table databases. Students interpret, model and visualise data using spreadsheets and database queries to draw conclusions. They select and use a range of digital tools to create, locate and communicate content, applying common conventions. Students use a range of digital tools to plan tasks, share content online, and manage individual and collaborative iterative projects. They manage their digital footprint and privacy when collecting data.

Students define and decompose real-world problems, and determine functional requirements and constraints. They design and trace algorithms using flowcharts and pseudocode. Students design and modify user interfaces and user experiences, and evaluate alternative designs. They implement algorithms and debug programs using a general-purpose programming language. Students evaluate digital solutions against the functional requirements.

Digital Citizenship

In this unit, students explore how they can be safe, responsible, and effective digital citizens. Through practical tasks and scenario-based activities, students develop an understanding of online safety, responsible use of school digital tools, and strategies for managing digital schoolwork, including their digital footprint and file organisation.

  • Multi-factor authentication and strong passwords

  • Trustworthy vs. untrustworthy online sources

  • Responsible use of school digital devices and platforms
  • Managing digital files and online schoolwork
  • Privacy settings and shared-content visibility

  • Communicate respectfully using collaboration tools

Timing Summary

Digital Citizenship (2wk) + Game Programming (4wk) + Robotics (3wk) + 3D Modelling (4wk) + The Internet (4wk) = 17 weeks, weeks 1–17. Week 18 is fixed for end-of-year activities. All 18 weeks are now allocated — there is no unscheduled buffer remaining in this revision.

Digital Data — statusIf revisited
Still deliberately out of scope for this revision. With weeks 15–17 now absorbed into Game Programming, Robotics, 3D Modelling and The Internet, there is no remaining gap for it to fill. A 2–3 week Digital Data unit (data types, spreadsheets, charting, survey design — VC2TDI8D01–06) would now need to displace part of another unit's time, or the course would need to run over more than 18 weeks. It previously existed as a fully-scoped unit in the original planner and could be restored from that content if a decision is made to bring it back.
