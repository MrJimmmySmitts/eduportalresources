LearningCore notes

What actually is the internet?

The internet is a giant network of networks — millions of computers and devices all over the world, connected together so they can share data. No single computer "is" the internet. It works because all those devices agree on a shared set of rules for sending information to each other.

📋 Copy this into your workbook — Section 2
Two ideas to remember 1. The internet is a network of networks.
2. The Internet and the World Wide Web are NOT the same thing. The Web (the websites you visit) is just one of the things that runs on the internet — others include email, online games and streaming.

A helpful analogy: the road network

In a road network……on the internet
RoadsThe cables and wireless signals that carry data
HousesDevices, like your laptop or phone
CarsThe data being delivered
Traffic rulesThe shared rules that keep data heading to the right place

LearningContext

A very short history

WhenWhat happened
1969ARPANET — the first version. A small US research network links a few university computers for the first time.
1989The Web is invented. Tim Berners-Lee designs the World Wide Web: web pages and clickable links.
1990sThe internet goes public. Home computers, dial-up modems and the first popular browsers bring it to ordinary households.
TodayAlways-on & everywhere. Phones, watches, TVs, fridges and cars — billions of devices connected, mostly wirelessly.

How it changed daily life

The good

Connect: message family overseas instantly.
Learn: look up anything, watch tutorials, online classes.
Create & share: post to a global audience.

The trade-offs

Screen time: easy to lose hours; affects sleep.
Not always true: anyone can post anything.
Always reachable: hard to switch off.

LearningCore notes

The parts of a network

📋 Copy this table into your workbook — Section 3
PartWhat it does
Device (client)Anything a person uses — laptop, phone, tablet, smart TV. It asks for data.
ServerA powerful computer that stores data and sends it out when devices ask. Websites live on servers.
RouterDirects data between networks and sends it to the right device — the "traffic controller".
ModemConnects your home network to the internet through your ISP.
SwitchConnects several devices together inside one local network (e.g. a computer lab).
ISPInternet Service Provider — the company you pay for an internet connection (e.g. Telstra).

How a request travels

Your device→ Router + Modem→ ISP→ The internet→ Website's server

When you open a website, your device sends a request out through your router and modem, to your ISP, across the internet to the server that stores that website. The server sends the data back along the same kind of path — all in a fraction of a second.

How does data travel?

📋 Copy this comparison into your workbook — Section 5
WIRED

Through a physical cable.

Ethernet (copper cable) — common in offices and labs.
Fibre optic — sends data as pulses of light through thin glass. Very fast; used by the NBN.

Strengths: fast, reliable, secure.
Catch: you're tied to a cable.

WIRELESS

Through the air as signals.

Wi-Fi — radio waves over short distances.
Mobile data (4G/5G) — phone towers; works almost anywhere.
Bluetooth — very short range. Satellite — remote areas.

Strengths: move around freely.
Catch: slower, weaker through walls/distance.

Extending challenge For any device, ask: does it move around? How far is it from the connection? How much speed does it need? Use those three questions to justify whether wired or wireless is the better choice.
